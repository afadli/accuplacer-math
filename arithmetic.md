## 1. Whole Number Operations & Word Problems

### Key Concepts
* **Order of Operations (PEMDAS):** Parentheses $\rightarrow$ Exponents $\rightarrow$ Multiplication & Division (left to right) $\rightarrow$ Addition & Subtraction (left to right).
* **Remainders in Word Problems:** If a problem involves dividing people or discrete items into containers or groups (e.g., buses, boxes, teams), you **must round up** to the next whole number if there is a remainder.
* **Estimation & Rounding:** Look one digit to the right of the target place value. If it is $5$ or greater, round up; if it is less than $5$, round down.

---

### Worked Examples

#### Example 1.1: Order of Operations
**Problem:** Evaluate $24 - 3 \times (8 - 4) + 2$
* **Step 1 (Parentheses):** $(8 - 4) = 4 \implies 24 - 3 \times 4 + 2$
* **Step 2 (Multiplication):** $3 \times 4 = 12 \implies 24 - 12 + 2$
* **Step 3 (Subtraction & Addition, left to right):** $24 - 12 = 12 \implies 12 + 2 = \mathbf{14}$

#### Example 1.2: Word Problem with Remainders
**Problem:** A school is taking 142 students on a field trip. Each van can hold a maximum of 12 students. How many vans are needed to transport all the students?
* **Calculation:** $142 \div 12 = 11 \text{ with a remainder of } 10$ ($11 \times 12 = 132$).
* **Logic:** 11 vans will hold $132$ students, leaving $10$ students behind. You need 1 more van for those remaining 10 students.
* **Answer:** **12 vans** (round up).

## 2. Fraction Operations

### Key Concepts
* **Adding & Subtracting:** Must find a common denominator first.
  $$\frac{a}{c} \pm \frac{b}{c} = \frac{a \pm b}{c}$$
* **Multiplying:** Multiply straight across (no common denominator needed).
  $$\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \times c}{b \times d}$$
* **Dividing (Keep-Change-Flip):** Keep the first fraction, change $\div$ to $\times$, and flip the second fraction.
  $$\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c}$$
* **Mixed Numbers to Improper:** $(\text{Whole} \times \text{Denominator}) + \text{Numerator} = \text{New Numerator}$.

---

### Worked Examples

#### Example 2.1: Adding Fractions
**Problem:** Evaluate $\frac{3}{4} + \frac{2}{3}$
* **Find Common Denominator:** LCD for $4$ and $3$ is $12$.
* **Convert:** $\frac{3 \times 3}{4 \times 3} = \frac{9}{12}$ and $\frac{2 \times 4}{3 \times 4} = \frac{8}{12}$
* **Add:** $\frac{9}{12} + \frac{8}{12} = \mathbf{\frac{17}{12}}$ (or $1\frac{5}{12}$)

#### Example 2.2: Dividing Fractions
**Problem:** Evaluate $\frac{5}{8} \div \frac{3}{4}$
* **Keep-Change-Flip:** $\frac{5}{8} \times \frac{4}{3}$
* **Multiply:** $\frac{5 \times 4}{8 \times 3} = \frac{20}{24}$
* **Simplify:** Divide top and bottom by $4 \implies \mathbf{\frac{5}{6}}$

## 3. Decimal Operations

### Key Concepts
* **Addition & Subtraction:** Line up the **decimal points** vertically. Pad empty spaces with zeros.
* **Multiplication:** Multiply normally. Count the *total* number of decimal places in the factors, and shift the decimal point that many places to the left in the product.
* **Division:** Move the divisor's decimal point to make it a whole number. Move the dividend's decimal point the **same number of spaces**, then bring the decimal point straight up.

---

### Worked Examples

#### Example 3.1: Multiplying Decimals
**Problem:** Find $2.5 \times 0.14$
* **Multiply as integers:** $25 \times 14 = 350$
* **Count decimal places:** $2.5$ (1 place) $+ 0.14$ (2 places) $= 3$ total places.
* **Shift decimal:** Move 3 places left from $350.0 \implies \mathbf{0.350}$ (or $0.35$)

#### Example 3.2: Dividing Decimals
**Problem:** Evaluate $3.6 \div 0.4$
* **Move divisor decimal:** $0.4 \rightarrow 4$ (moved 1 place right).
* **Move dividend decimal:** $3.6 \rightarrow 36$ (moved 1 place right).
* **Divide:** $36 \div 4 = \mathbf{9}$

## 4. Percentages

### Key Concepts
* **The Core Formula:** $\text{Part} = \text{Decimal Percent} \times \text{Whole}$
* **Converting Percent to Decimal:** Move the decimal point **two places to the left** (e.g., $8.5\% \rightarrow 0.085$).
* **Finding "What Percent...":** $\text{Percent} = \left( \frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Whole}} \right) \times 100$

---

### Worked Examples

#### Example 4.1: Finding a Percent of a Number
**Problem:** What is $15\%$ of $80$?
* **Convert percent:** $15\% = 0.15$
* **Multiply:** $0.15 \times 80 = \mathbf{12}$

#### Example 4.2: Finding What Percent One Number is of Another
**Problem:** A store sold 18 out of 45 items on sale. What percentage of items were sold?
* **Set up fraction:** $\frac{18}{45} = \frac{2}{5} = 0.4$
* **Convert to percent:** $0.4 \times 100 = \mathbf{40\%}$

## 5. Number Comparisons & Equivalents

### Key Concepts
* **The Universal Strategy:** When asked to order or compare mixed formats (fractions, decimals, percentages), **convert everything into decimals** first to evaluate them on an equal playing field.
* **Fraction to Decimal:** Divide numerator by denominator (Top $\div$ Bottom).

---

### Worked Examples

#### Example 5.1: Ordering Mixed Values
**Problem:** Order the following from least to greatest: $\frac{3}{5}$, $0.62$, and $58\%$.
* **Convert $\frac{3}{5}$ to decimal:** $3 \div 5 = 0.60$
* **Keep $0.62$ as decimal:** $0.62$
* **Convert $58\%$ to decimal:** $0.58$
* **Compare Decimals:** $0.58 < 0.60 < 0.62$
* **Final Ordered List:** $\mathbf{58\%, \ \frac{3}{5}, \ 0.62}$
